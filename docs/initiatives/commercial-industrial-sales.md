## Business outcome and positioning

P1 will pursue larger, longer-lived commercial and industrial relationships as an **Exterior Site Operations & Land Management Partner**. The value proposition is **“One accountable partner for everything outside the building envelope.”** Supporting copy must qualify that positioning with confirmed service scopes and the customer's operating requirements; it must not imply that P1 performs every regulated trade or guarantees uninterrupted operations.

The initiative supports new-business development, account qualification, property assessments, procurement and retention. Success is qualified opportunities progressing into assessed properties, accepted proposals and recurring work—not landing-page traffic alone. Existing services and customer segments remain supported; this adds a commercial sales path without deleting or weakening them.

## Property lifecycle and assessment offer

| Stage | Commercial relationship |
|---|---|
| Development / Construction | Site support, clearing, vegetation control, erosion/corrective work, drainage, grading, temporary/access-area maintenance and post-construction restoration within confirmed scope |
| Turnover / Establishment | Establishment support, perimeter/buffer management, initial cleanup, baseline property assessment and recurring-service planning |
| Long-Term Operations | Grounds, vegetation, water systems, trees, roads/access, inspections, corrective projects and agreed response arrangements |

Begin relationships before facilities open and retain continuity through turnover. Development stage, next milestone and expected opening inform sales timing; they do not substitute for an approved schedule or service contract.

**P1 Commercial Site Assessment** is the primary sales offer. Intake qualifies the property, requested scope, location, project stage and operational fit. Staff then confirms availability, access requirements, assessment scope, any fee and the property walk. The offer is not represented as an engineering certification, compliance audit or emergency dispatch service.

Assessment scope may cover grounds condition, vegetation, trees, drainage/stormwater, erosion, ponds, access roads, deferred maintenance and potential problem areas. Planned deliverable: dated, reviewed baseline with property/area references, photos, observations, priorities, recommended actions and a proposed recurring/corrective work plan. Preserve observed facts separately from estimates and recommendations. Only approved reports become client-visible; operational evidence never becomes public marketing automatically.

Recommended workflow:

`Commercial visitor → accepted inquiry → qualified lead → company/contact links → prospect property/project → confirmed assessment → reviewed assessment → opportunity/proposal → won client relationship → property onboarding → recurring services/work orders → inspections/issues/history/reporting → renewal/expansion`

Some steps overlap: an existing account or property should be linked rather than recreated, and an early account pursuit can exist before a physical property is known. Booking, qualification, proposal approval and client access require their own explicit actions.

Use the existing managed-form foundation. Proposed form slug: `p1-commercial-assessment`; proposed POST remains under `/api/forms/:slug/submit`. Final OpenAPI/Zod contract and Core field compatibility must be reviewed before code. Preserve existing `source=website_form`; add structured `inquiryType=commercial_site_assessment`, service/industry/project-stage fields and permitted attribution rather than overloading the source or CRM stage.

| Intake step | Fields and UX |
|---|---|
| Initial request | Name, company, email or phone, property location (address or city/region if not yet known), services/need, immediate/recurring/both, project stage. Require one usable contact channel, not both. |
| Optional qualification | Title, other contact channel, property/project name, property type, acreage or unknown/range, fuller address and message. Keep these available without turning every field into a blocker. |
| Secure follow-up | Plans/documents, property photos and site maps when the protected attachment workflow is ready. Do not ask for access codes, facility-security plans or unnecessary sensitive details in the public form. |

Support progressive disclosure, accessible field labels/errors/autofill, mobile completion, retained inputs and a stable retry key. Success means durable receipt, with an inquiry reference and honest next step; it does not mean email delivery, an appointment or a service commitment. Contact clicks remain separate from accepted inquiries.

**Ownership contract:** P1 Core owns the website submission and initial intake receipt. Save submission and bridge-outbox work transactionally. A signed/authenticated server-to-server delivery sends a versioned, bounded commercial-inquiry event to the dashboard's private integration boundary. The dashboard owns qualified account/property sales and operations. It must commit an inbox receipt, lead/link creation and audit atomically before acknowledging. Use a unique source-system/submission/event identity, payload fingerprint, version and durable retry/recovery state. No browser secret, direct database join, public dashboard intake duplicate, or second browser POST.

Retried delivery must return the original mapping; the same contact submitting a different project creates a distinct inquiry. Link/merge companies and contacts only through explicit matching rules and review—shared email domain is not sufficient. Keep Core's New → Contacted → Qualified → Proposal → Won/Lost intake pipeline; map richer dashboard pursuit states explicitly, rather than silently replacing those stages. Once linked, the dashboard owns ongoing opportunity stage; any Core summary update must be a separately idempotent projection with defined ownership, not a two-way edit loop.

Staff need commercial queue ownership, assignment, next action, last activity, receipt-to-follow-up timing, failed-delivery recovery and an audit trail. A dashboard outage or Mailgun failure must not lose a request. Notification is a delivery channel, not the lead store. Use first-party attribution and existing Mailgun direction; no new analytics subscription or unsolicited outreach automation is included.

Provide structured, versioned editing for hero/supporting copy, CTA labels/targets, ordered capability groups, one-partner visual/text, lifecycle stages, industries, data-center copy, assessment process, service coverage, approved project-story references and SEO/social fields. Reuse layout components; avoid unrestricted HTML. Register `/commercial` in route/content contracts, sitemap and route checks only when implemented; preserve reserved infrastructure paths.

Add **private admin placeholders** for credentials/insurance/certification, equipment/capacity, safety process, documentation/reporting samples, onboarding/vendor information, W-9/COI availability, response arrangements, case studies, before/after photos and permitted client logos. Empty/unverified sections are hidden publicly. Proof records need source, technical reviewer, review/expiry dates, permission status/scope and publication history. A document's possession is not publication permission. Procurement files should be access-controlled or provided on request, not exposed merely because CMS media exists.

When an approved public copy update changes an existing CMS default, preserve its field identity so saved drafts and published revisions remain editable and renderable. The public adapter keeps a reviewed key-alias registry for this purpose; a key may only carry forward for the same source field, never a different content location.

Use modern, premium industrial/exterior-site design within P1's visual identity. Favor large maintained acreage, campuses, heavy equipment, water infrastructure, vegetation/site work and aerial views. Use authentic permitted photography where available; clearly label illustrative imagery and never imply stock imagery is P1's work. Keep public/dashboard bundles separate. Preserve hero≤250KB, cards≤100KB, total initial assets≤1.5MB and initial JS≤150KB gzip; verify keyboard, screen reader, reflow, contrast, form states and reduced motion.

Secure optional uploads should follow durable intake, with expiring submission-bound authorization, strict type/byte/pixel limits, malware quarantine for documents, private storage, authenticated staff access and safe downloads. Do not reuse public gallery URLs for campus plans. This is a gated enhancement, not a blocker to launching a useful text-only commercial inquiry form.

## Recommendations and outcome measurement

1. Offer a clear assessment deliverable and next step rather than an undifferentiated quote request. Explain what P1 needs, what staff will confirm and what the buyer receives.
2. Show how P1 coordinates exterior responsibilities with documented scope and exclusions. Use genuine process/report examples, not unsupported enterprise badges.
3. Support portfolios and multiple stakeholder roles without granting cross-property access by association. Large owners and facilities managers often span several sites.
4. Preserve a property baseline from assessment through recurring work; make sales promises, exclusions and approved findings visible to the operational team. This reduces handoff loss and helps expansion/renewal conversations.
5. Make response ownership and next action visible before adding complex sales automation. Track overdue inquiries and delivery failures with an accountable staff owner.
6. Provide a gated procurement pack and capability statement once reviewed. Do not put sensitive insurance/tax/customer/site documents on a public CDN by default.
7. Use data-center relevance through verified applicable scopes and professional coordination. Broaden to manufacturing/logistics and other target properties without implying unproven sector experience.

Measure form starts, accepted commercial inquiries, qualification rate, response/next-action timeliness, requested versus booked/completed assessments, proposals, wins, recurring agreements, renewal and service expansion. Segment by source, service, property type/industry and confirmed market; keep contact details out of analytics. Track estimated P1 opportunity value separately from accepted contract value and actual accounting revenue. Set targets after baseline and capacity review; do not manufacture conversion-lift promises.
