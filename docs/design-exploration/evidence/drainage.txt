  - heading "Start with Where the Water Goes" [level=2]
  - paragraph: Poor drainage is one of the most common — and most costly — problems facing large property owners in the Carolinas. Heavy clay soils, compacted ground, improper grading, and flat topography create conditions where water sits, roots suffocate, structures erode, and maintenance costs multiply.
  - paragraph: We'll trace where water enters, where it collects, and where it needs to drain. Then we'll explain the work we recommend and any engineering, permits, or specialist help needed before installation.
  - img "Drainage system being installed to move water off a property"
  - heading "Drainage Services" [level=2]
  - list:
    - generic: French drain installation — perforated pipe and gravel systems to intercept and redirect subsurface water
    - generic: Surface drainage swales — graded channels to carry surface runoff away from structures and fields
    - generic: Retention and detention pond planning and construction
    - generic: Catch basin and inlet installation
    - generic: Underground drainage pipe installation
    - generic: Agricultural drainage — tile drainage for fields and pastures
    - generic: Ditch clearing, shaping, and lining
    - generic: Regrading for drainage correction
    - generic: Erosion control and slope stabilization in drainage zones
    - generic: Stormwater maintenance and service records
  - heading "Common Drainage Problems We Solve" [level=2]
  - list:
    - listitem: Standing water in fields, commercial grounds, or near structures after rain
    - listitem: Soggy, compacted soil that won't grow healthy turf
    - listitem: Erosion on slopes, embankments, or around ponds
    - listitem: Flooded access roads or driveways
    - listitem: Drainage ditches that are silted, blocked, or undersized
    - listitem: Retention ponds overflowing or failing to drain properly
    - listitem: Wet basements or foundations on large rural properties
  - heading "Agricultural Drainage" [level=2]
  - paragraph: For farm and agricultural properties, drainage is directly tied to productivity. Saturated fields can't be worked, can't support healthy root systems, and lose topsoil to runoff. P1 installs agricultural drainage systems — including field tile drainage and perimeter ditching — that improve field trafficability and long-term productivity.
