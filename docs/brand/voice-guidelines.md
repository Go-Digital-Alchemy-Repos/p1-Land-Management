# P1 Website Voice Guidelines

Approved by the Owner on September 17, 2026. Applies to public website copy and future CMS edits.

Write like an experienced owner walking a property with its manager: approachable, direct, and comfortable explaining the work. Professionalism comes from useful details, not corporate vocabulary.

- Use we and you naturally. Answer the question first, then explain.
- Name the work: mow, clear, grade, repair, drain, plant, maintain.
- Keep technical terms when useful; explain unfamiliar ones.
- Use contractions and varied, readable sentences. Give each paragraph a useful purpose.
- Write headings in title case and body copy in sentence case.
- Say what is included, when the crew can come, and who to call. Avoid repeated references to scope, qualification, mobilization, property lifecycle, and coordination.
- Keep verified capabilities confident. State real limits plainly beside the affected service. Preserve the one-acre minimum and commercial, industrial, agricultural, municipal, and institutional focus.
- Keep local details useful. Do not repeat the full business name or customer-category list in every paragraph.
- Avoid contractor comparisons, swagger, invented guarantees, and unsupported numerical claims.
- Keep the main button text: Get a Free Site Assessment.
- Commercial facility pages can explain access, vendor paperwork, reporting, and scheduling in the same human voice.

Example: “Whether you're clearing land, fixing drainage, or keeping the grounds in shape, we can help. Start with the work you need now, and we'll help you plan what comes next.”

Before publishing, read the copy as a conversation. Check that service limits, contact details, links, metadata, and FAQ structured data still match. Update both CMS manifests whenever default copy changes.
